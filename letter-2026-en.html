A First Letter to Our Users

November 1, 2026 · Du株

To all our users,

Before we begin

AYS (Awaken YourSelf) is a health-education project built around one idea: the human body's capacity to recover. It's only been running for half a month. That's only half true, though — the idea first came to me late last year. It just stayed vague. I couldn't work out what exactly I wanted to do. These days almost any idea you have seems like someone else has already done it, and the odds of success look pretty grim…

Because this is only just starting, this first letter is less about answers than about the why, and the general direction — so that over the coming year I can keep moving without drifting off course.

As for why a letter at all: I'm borrowing the habit from Buffett's and Bezos's shareholder letters. Once a year they write one, and in doing so they anchor themselves to where the company ought to stand, so that capital can't pull it off course. Of course, I'm just an ordinary person.

Why this, of all things?

Why recovery, why health? Because along the way — through studying and through practice — I genuinely changed. Those changes brought me a calm mind and a kind of fulfillment I hadn't known, and let me feel the honest feedback of my own body. They took me away from obesity, from smoking, from late nights — away from a numb, drifting stretch of my life I'd rather not look back on.

Wanting to build this also comes from how I've spent these years. Since leaving school I've been a programmer, a product manager, a project manager, an operations lead, a team lead — and then I found nutrition and started changing myself. I've always been someone who wants to share. Meet me in person and you might think I'm quiet; but I love passing on good things, and I want to meet more people walking the same road.

What I've learned since leaving school has been more, and more useful, than anything I read at university. That's down to the people around me — the mentors who brought me along, and the friends who stayed (a special thank-you to the owner of "Coffee the Dog") — structural thinking, product thinking, operations, economics, value investing… each one has quietly reshaped how I see things.

And all of it, every bit, I've poured into this project, holding to three principles — the long view, honesty, and openness — to make something genuinely worthwhile and lasting. For now it's just one person's curiosity given a try. I hope I get to keep doing it.

What is recovery?

On healthy eating and healthy living, in an age with this much video, this much advice, this much AI, I'd guess you already know most of it.

But for those of us up against the ordinary mess of real life, following the science to the letter — eating, sleeping, moving exactly as told — is genuinely hard. Very few people can run their days to a perfect schedule with nothing slipping.

So here's what I want to offer — knowledge, products, a way of thinking. For ordinary people who will, now and then, stay up too late, go out and enjoy themselves, have barbecue and beer, and let themselves off the leash: the point is to adjust habits and understanding slowly, almost without noticing, so that body and mind both keep a dependable capacity to recover — so that you get back to your own metabolism, your own mental state, and your own weight balance faster than the next person.

That's what this project is for: to help you gain that capacity, and keep it.

A comparison:

  • Same all-nighter: one person looks hollowed out the next day, barely functioning; another catches up on sleep, eats a proper meal, and by evening is back among the living;
  • Same night of barbecue and beer: one person spends three days bloated and off their food, and the scale refuses to move; another eats and moves as usual, and within two or three days is back in their own rhythm;
  • Same seasonal cold: one drags it out for two weeks; another is back at full strength in about a week.

Recovery isn't about never falling. It's about how fast you come back after you do. It rests on the small, unremarkable habits — sleeping a little more regularly, eating a little cleaner, staying up a little less. When life knocks you out of rhythm, it brings you back to yourself sooner.

So how will I do it?

Honestly? I don't know either.

Going by the "project introduction" you've seen, it's a big goal, and a vague one. But I'm still willing to spend my spare hours thinking it through and doing it with everything I have — to try every angle I can think of.

For now, I'll start with content services…

In 2027, I hope to write you another one…
